## 📖 Overview

<details>
<summary><b>Overview</b></summary>

KIDA v00 is the foundational entry in the Kinetic series, built on the Raspberry Pi 3 Model B. v00 is a versatile teleoperated and autonomous scout designed for robust remote monitoring and computer vision tasks.

Core Features
- [x] Multi-Protocol Control: Seamless operation via Web Interface, VNC, or direct Remote Control.
- [x] Live Surveillance: High-definition video streaming with on-demand image and video capture.
- [x] Biometric Inference: Edge-based vision processing for real-time Gender and Age detection.
- [x] Reactive Autonomy: Integrated Ultrasonic obstacle avoidance and high-contrast line-following logic.
- [x] Wall Guard: she won't drive forward into anything within 15 cm, whatever is driving her (keyboard, tank, gamepad, web, line following); turning and reversing still work, so she can always get away (`motor_control.WALL_GUARD_M`).
- [x] Visual Feedback: Customizable RGB lighting for status signaling and environmental illumination.

</details>

## 🤖 HELLO, DANCE and fleet talk

- **Android app (`Android App/`, package `com.synthwomb`): FIND gets her address from the fleet list, or type it in. Hold the stick to drive, let go to stop, and her modes, HELLO, DANCE, photo, music and the wheel lock are buttons. Build it with `gradlew assembleDebug`.**
- **HELLO and DANCE:** on the spot, in USER mode: she says it, her camera shakes, and DANCE turns her left and right. A stop or the wheel lock ends it. **J** and **N** on the PC controller, buttons on her dashboard, `hello` and `dance` in `POST /do`.
- **Brainfuck out loud:** `/chirp?u=<n>` plays the fleet's phrases on her speaker (`scripts/chirp.py`), so RIFT's conversations include her.
- `/status` carries `rev`, her settings in one number.

## 🧠 MIND mode: an LLM drives her

Her PC controller's LLM drives her, stop-look-think-move: she stands, looks, thinks (you can read her thinking on the controller), says something in her own voice from her own speaker, and makes one short move. The mind runs on the PC (`controller/drive_mind.py`, the fleet's shared `scripts/mind.py`); her Pi only does what it's asked.

- **Which PC:** an x86 Windows or Linux PC with 8 GB+ RAM and a GTX 1050 or better, with [Ollama](https://ollama.com). The model is sized to the GPU (qwen3). Add a vision model (`ollama pull gemma3:4b-it-qat`) and she sees with her camera too.
- **Start it:** run the controller (`controller/run.bat <her-ip>`), then press <kbd>9</kbd> there or on her. Any other mode (<kbd>U</kbd>, <kbd>O</kbd>, <kbd>L</kbd>, the gamepad's B) ends it, and so does closing the controller: she stands after 5 s without it.
- **Looking around:** her camera is on a servo, so one of her moves is `look_around`. She turns her camera left, ahead and right, and the vision model lists what's at each side. She remembers it for a minute, and her camera is centred again before she drives.
- **Her reflexes stay on:** the wheel lock (signing in as her mind never unlocks it), and the wall guard: she won't drive forward into anything within 15 cm.
- **The PIN:** the controller signs in with her `MotorPassword` (`KIDA_PIN` on the PC), or `1234` while that's empty.

Her side of it is `scripts/mind_drive.py`: `/unlock?pw=&ctl=1`, `/status?ctl=1`, `/mode?m=mind`, `/mind?act=fwd|bwd|left|right|stop&steps=1-4`, `/look?at=left|centre|right` (a JPEG) and `POST /speak` (a WAV).

## 📡 Who's nearby (ESP-NOW + Bluetooth LE)

Every robot sends a small **"I'm here"** beacon twice a second and listens for the others'. From the **signal strength** it knows roughly how close each one is, and from how that changes over time whether it's **coming closer, steady or leaving**:

| Signal | Zone |
| :--- | :--- |
| above −45 dBm | very close |
| −45 to −60 dBm | near |
| −60 to −75 dBm | medium |
| below −75 dBm | far |

It's coarse (walls, bodies and antenna angle all change it): for "who's around", not distance. Precise collision avoidance stays with the ultrasonic and ToF sensors.

**They tell each other what they're doing**, because a rising signal looks the same from both sides even when only one robot moves. Over ESP-NOW they say it **in Brainfuck**, like the fleet's conversations: each beacon carries a program that prints `park`, `go`, `wait` or `hand` (a human is driving), and the receiver runs it. Bluetooth adverts are too small for a program, so BLE carries the same state as one byte.

**Who makes way**, in self-driving modes only:

| The other robot... | So this one... |
| :--- | :--- |
| is parked | is the one closing in: steers away |
| is yielding | carries on, carefully |
| is driven by a human | makes way (it's unpredictable) |
| drives itself | follows the alphabet: KIDA00, KIDA01, NORA, WHIP; everyone makes way for MILA, who can't hear the others |
| is leaving | carries on |

Making way = stop for 2 s, turn away, then drive on (and not yield again for 5 s, so two robots that stay close don't take turns forever). While another robot is near, or coming closer, it drives slower with wider margins.

A Pi can't speak ESP-NOW, so KIDA-00 uses the Pi's own **Bluetooth LE**: she beacons through `bluetoothctl` and listens with `bleak`, hearing NORA, WHIP, KIDA-01 and RIFT. In **autonomous** mode she drives at 60 % of your speed while a robot is near and gives way when one with right of way is close; in **line** mode she waits. `GET /near` shows her list. Code: `scripts/fleet_near.py`.
